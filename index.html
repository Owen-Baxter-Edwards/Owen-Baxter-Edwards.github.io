01About & Career Bridge

I came to analytics by way of economics, not around it. My undergraduate training in quantitative economics and statistics at NC State taught me to ask rigorous causal questions — why did credit delinquency move the way it did, why did informative signal drop out of disaster Twitter data — and to answer them with data. The Wake Forest MSBA is the deliberate next step: it takes that econometric foundation and points it at business problems, teaching me the tools (Python, SQL, applied machine learning, stakeholder communication) that let the same rigor drive commercial decisions, not just academic papers. I'm not starting over in analytics; I'm translating.

That's the throughline of this hybrid track: everything below maps a skill I already built in economics, research, or leadership onto the specific capability employers in data and business analytics are hiring for right now.

Hurricane Helene Twitter Informativeness Classification

PythonCNN / NLPMatplotlibPandas
Context

During Hurricane Helene, emergency responders faced an overwhelming, unfiltered stream of social media posts, making it hard to separate actionable information from noise in real time — a version of the "data volume without data quality" problem that now ranks among the top blockers to data-driven decision-making across industries.

My Role

As one of 3 researchers in the CADS Lab, I built and trained a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) text classifier to label tweets as informative or noninformative, engineered comparative visualizations in Python (Matplotlib, Pandas) across the disaster's response and recovery phases, and co-authored the final research paper.

Outcome
Quantified a 20 percentage-point drop in informative tweet share between response and recovery phases — a concrete, presentable signal disaster-response teams can use to recalibrate social-listening effort as a crisis unfolds.

Generational Credit Card Delinquency Analysis

RRegressionFederal Reserve Data
Context

Rising and unevenly distributed consumer debt is a live concern for lenders and regulators trying to price risk and design policy across generational cohorts.

My Role

I independently sourced and cleaned multi-year datasets from several Federal Reserve credit data resources, built a first-differences regression model isolating macroeconomic drivers of delinquency, and visualized results by generational cohort.

Outcome
Found that unemployment rate and real disposable income were statistically significant predictors of delinquency — with a materially larger effect on younger generations — a finding directly relevant to consumer-credit risk modeling and generational lending strategy.
